This document contains fictional information about NetWave networking equipment and customer support. It reflects a combination of product descriptions, support-team observations, and policy guidance. Model names, stock identifiers, specifications, prices, and support policies are invented for retrieval practice.

A customer recently asked whether the Aether Mini would be enough for a compact apartment or whether the more expensive Aether X7 would offer a meaningful advantage. Both devices provide wireless connectivity on the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands, but they are not interchangeable. The Mini is a Wi-Fi 5 router sold for ₹3,499 under SKU-7742M. Its WAN connection is Gigabit-capable, whereas the two LAN ports operate at Fast Ethernet speeds, meaning a wired device connected to either LAN port cannot negotiate a link above 100 Mbps. The Mini includes a guest network and basic parental controls. The standard warranty is twelve months. It has no advanced per-device traffic prioritization, a distinction that matters whenever a customer wants to reduce lag caused by competing traffic.

The Aether X7, identified as SKU-7742X, occupies a different position in the range. It costs ₹8,999 and is intended for households with many devices, video meetings, gaming sessions, and simultaneous 4K streams. Unlike the Mini, it supports Wi-Fi 6, WPA3 security, advanced Quality of Service controls, a guest network, and parental controls. One Gigabit WAN port and four Gigabit LAN ports are available, and its warranty extends to twenty-four months. In the router dashboard, a user who wants to favor a gaming console or conferencing laptop can open Network > QoS and assign that device a high priority. Support staff should not direct Mini owners to that advanced per-device setting because the feature is specific to the X7. The similarity between the two SKU strings has caused incorrect recommendations before: the last character, X or M, identifies a substantially different product.

The most common report from an X7 owner with slow games or unstable video calls is that performance becomes worse when other household members are streaming. The first useful distinction is between congestion and weak Wi-Fi. A technician can ask the customer to test speeds through one of the X7's Gigabit LAN ports, then try a closer wireless position and the 5 GHz band. Updating firmware from System > Firmware Update may also be appropriate. If the connection otherwise works but a game or call suffers while another device saturates the upload, the advanced QoS setting under Network > QoS is relevant. Checking competing upload activity remains useful even when traffic prioritization is enabled. These steps should not be copied unchanged into guidance for SKU-7742M, whose feature set is more limited.

A different support case involves an X7 that broadcasts a working Wi-Fi network but cannot open websites. A red internet-status LED is one recognizable symptom. A local wireless connection does not establish that the upstream internet connection is healthy. Support generally starts by confirming that the modem or optical network terminal has power and internet service, and that its Ethernet lead enters the X7's WAN port rather than one of its LAN ports. If connections appear correct, the modem can be restarted first; after roughly two minutes, the router can be restarted. Internet > WAN Status in the dashboard shows whether the router received a WAN IP address. If no address appears, the customer may need their ISP to verify service and authentication settings. A factory reset is not the initial remedy: the existing ISP configuration should be recorded before any such reset is considered.

Extending wireless coverage presents a separate product decision. NetWave sells a dual-band Wi-Fi 6 device named Beacon E2 for ₹4,299, associated with SKU-8801E. It provides roaming assistance, Ethernet backhaul, and mesh pairing when the main router is compatible. Its warranty is eighteen months. The E2 is intended to improve reception in a larger home, but placing it inside a room with no usable wireless signal is counterproductive. A sensible initial location is approximately halfway between the main router and the troublesome room. Placement for the initial pairing operation is different from placement for everyday coverage: the extender should first be within three meters of the router.

One recurring source of confusion is that a Beacon E2 can work with an Aether Mini as an ordinary wireless extender but cannot form a mesh pair with that router. Mesh operation with the E2 is supported by the Aether X7. When a pairing indicator continues to blink for more than five minutes, the support team should establish whether the customer is attempting mesh mode or ordinary extender operation and check the router model. On a compatible X7 setup, the extender's pairing button is held for three seconds and pairing is started from Network > Mesh Devices in the X7 dashboard. Once the devices pair, the E2 can be moved toward the area needing improved coverage. A failure to establish mesh mode with the Mini is a compatibility limitation, not evidence that the E2 must be replaced.

Ethernet expansion is handled by another device rather than a wireless extender. The CoreSwitch 8G, SKU-9008G, is a plug-and-play eight-port Gigabit Ethernet switch priced at ₹2,199. It supports auto-negotiation and carries a thirty-six-month warranty. Its purpose is to add wired connections for equipment such as PCs, consoles, and cameras. One switch port connects to a LAN port on an existing router, with the other ports available for devices. The switch does not assign IP addresses and cannot substitute for a router. It works alongside both the X7 and the Mini, although the router attached upstream can constrain traffic to and from the wider network.

This distinction matters in reports that an Ethernet connection is stuck at 100 Mbps. A customer using the Mini cannot obtain a Gigabit link from either of its built-in LAN ports, even if the connected laptop and cable support faster speeds. An X7 or CoreSwitch 8G, by contrast, offers Gigabit-capable Ethernet ports. When those devices negotiate only 100 Mbps, support should inspect which port is in use, the network adapter's settings, and the cable. Cat5e or better cabling is appropriate, and replacing a damaged cable may resolve negotiation problems. A CoreSwitch linked to a Mini may still provide Gigabit links between devices attached to the switch, but traffic using the Mini's 100 Mbps LAN uplink remains constrained by that uplink. A switch cannot make the Mini's physical LAN interface faster.

NetWave's sales and service rules apply across these products, though particular hardware has its own warranty duration. Direct purchases from the NetWave online store can be returned within fourteen calendar days after delivery, provided the original invoice and accessories are available. The normal return policy does not cover physical damage due to misuse. After inspection, a refund is generally initiated within five business days, but payment providers may take additional time to credit the customer. The warranty-claim process is different from a return request: it requires the invoice and product serial number. Liquid damage and unauthorized repairs are excluded from the standard warranty.

Shipping depends on the order value and service selected. Within India, standard delivery is estimated at three to seven business days, while express delivery, where available, is estimated at one to three business days. NetWave provides free standard shipping when the order total is above ₹5,000. A total of ₹5,000 exactly does not qualify because the rule says above, rather than at least. Standard shipping costs ₹199 for orders at or below that threshold. For example, a standalone X7 meets the free-shipping threshold, whereas a standalone Mini does not; the selected shipping method and full order total still matter.

Product comparisons can lead to misleading shortcuts. An E2 mesh extender and a CoreSwitch both add some form of connectivity, but they solve different problems. The E2 helps cover wireless areas and supports mesh operation only with a compatible main router such as the X7. A CoreSwitch adds wired Gigabit ports but does not improve a router's Wi-Fi range. Likewise, a router's Wi-Fi generation should not be inferred from a product identifier. The X7 is specified as Wi-Fi 6 and the Mini as Wi-Fi 5; there is no claim here that the X7 supports Wi-Fi 7. The source material does not identify the manufacturer of the X7's processor or chipset.

Before escalating a connectivity problem, support personnel should record the precise SKU, firmware version, WAN status, LED state, and troubleshooting already attempted. Requests involving replacement should include the serial number, invoice date, and a description of the defect. Customer Wi-Fi passwords must never be requested in a support ticket. These details are useful for an investigation regardless of whether the initial complaint concerns an intermittent wireless connection, a missing WAN IP address, an unsuccessful extender pairing, or a slow wired link.
